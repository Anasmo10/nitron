# Content principles

Audience: شباب وصبايا بسوريا بين 18 و35 سنة، بيتمرنوا حديد بنادي من 6 أشهر لسنتين. بيشتروا إكسسوارات
(ستراب، رباط معصم، كفوف، حزام) بس أغلبهن ما بيعرفوا إمتى بتلزم وكيف بتنلبس صح. بيتفرجوا عالموبايل
بين الجولات أو بآخر الليل، وأكتر شي بيعملوه بفيديو عجبهن: بيبعتوه لرفيق التمرين.
Language and tone: عربي لهجة سورية — متل مدرّب صاحبك بالنادي: مباشر، بلا فصحى وبلا مبالغة.

## Principles (each with its reason)
1. Line one sells line two. Reason: nobody keeps watching without a reason in the first seconds.
2. A precise detail beats a general claim. Reason: specifics make it believable.
3. Open a question early and answer it at the end. Reason: an open question keeps people watching.
4. One idea per script. Reason: two ideas dilute both.
5. Start from a situation people live. Reason: they recognise themselves before they hear the information.
6. End on the payoff, not on filler.
7. Every video leaves the viewer one thing to try in their next workout. Reason: this audience trains
   3–5 times a week; a video they can test gets saved and sent to a training partner, and sends carry it
   to people who don't follow us yet.
8. The product appears as the tool that fixes the problem, in the middle — never as an ad at the end.
   Reason: this audience scrolls past ads in the first second, but accepts a tool once they've seen the
   problem it solves.
9. The hook is a specific claim that contradicts what they believe, or a shot they've never seen —
   never a generic question ("رسغك بيوجعك؟"). Reason: generic questions sound like every other fitness
   reel; feedback on batch 1 was "no hook".
10. No line that announces, recaps or stalls ("شوف"، "رح قلّك بالآخر"، "بخمس ثواني"، the same "بتمرينك الجاي"
    closer in every video). Every line adds a new fact or a new action; 4–6 lines, under 20 seconds.
    Reason: one empty second is enough to lose them; feedback on batch 1 was "full of filler".

## How to use
- Before writing, state which principles the script will lean on.
- After writing, check every line against the list and fix what fails.
- Never copy the structure of a reference post. Take only the reason it works.
- Any rule, number or name on screen needs a source (federation rulebook, the product itself, or what the
  presenter says on camera). No source → say it in general terms instead.

## Workflow when given a video idea or a product
These house principles win over any generic skill. The other skills are tools for one step each.

1. **Audience and goal.** Name the viewer (from Audience above) and ONE goal: reach, sends, saves, or a sale.
2. **References.** If search or reel files are available, study 2–3 real reels (`viral-reverse-engineering`).
   Use only what can be seen: transcript, frames, visible counts. Never claim to have watched a reel from a
   link alone. Label every pattern taken from them as **مستند** (seen in a reference) or **فرضية** (a creative
   guess to test).
3. **Three different ideas**: three different mechanisms (e.g. contradiction, demo, mistake→fix), not three
   wordings of one idea.
4. **Hooks for each idea.** Verbal hook system from `hooksmith` (spoken line + on-screen text + open loop +
   rehook). Visual hook from `virality`: frame 0 already moving, what is on screen every 0.5 s up to 3 s.
5. **Final script with timings** for the chosen idea (`reels-script`): a row per beat — time · shot ·
   spoken · on-screen text · audio. Muted version must still make sense.
6. **Shoot and edit plan**: shot list grouped by setup, angle, camera move, light, sound, transitions
   (`scripting-and-storyboarding`, `talking-head-and-piece-to-camera`).
7. **Why, and what to test**: one reason per choice, then 1–2 A/B tests (e.g. two hooks as Trial Reels),
   each with the metric that decides it (3-second hold, average watch time, sends, saves).

For paid ads or a direct sale, add `ad-maker`: pick the angle from its angle × awareness matrix before
writing the hook. Organic videos still follow principle 8 (product in the middle, no ad ending).
